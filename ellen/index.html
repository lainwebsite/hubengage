Full case study

The challenge

AI regulatory requirements made continued use of Zoom unviable because the platform retained company data, requiring Northwestern Mutual to build its own compliant enterprise solution for 200,000+ Sales and Recruiting users. The new platform also needed to support transcription, summarization, coaching logic, and monetization insights across both functions.

At the same time, requirements had stalled on a disputed question: what confidence threshold was safe enough for AI-generated coaching insights to reach advisors directly versus requiring human review first. Leadership resistance was slowing progress, and governance sessions with 5–8 representatives per function were producing debate instead of decisions.

The mission

Deliver a unified program spanning two connected GenAI releases: clear ownership, enterprise-grade infrastructure, and a repeatable framework for measuring and improving model performance across the organization.

Restore stakeholder confidence, break the organizational deadlock, and stand up a governance model capable of sustaining AI investment decisions long-term.

The approach

  • Restructured governance in the first month: cut participation to 2–3 subject-matter authorities per function, instituted daily working sessions, and built a data-driven progress model contrasting actual pace against the original roadmap to align leadership around urgency.
  • Finalized requirements in three weeks, a deliverable that by itself unlocked Engineering and Data Science’s ability to begin architectural planning despite their initial refusal to commit.
  • Partnered with the CEO on two enterprise AI cost initiatives: tiered model routing that directs simple queries to lower-cost models, then a full vendor-migration feasibility analysis prompted by a mandated 30% spend reduction.
  • Established the governing framework (program charter, RACI, integrated roadmap) aligning Sales and HR as end users with Engineering, Data Science, Finance and Legal as domain stakeholders.
  • Resolved the confidence-threshold debate by setting a 75% confidence bar for AI-generated outputs: above it, insights surfaced directly to advisors; below it, outputs routed to human-in-the-loop review.
  • Directed delivery of NM’s first GenAI product on AWS Bedrock: an in-house transcription tool feeding an event-driven analysis layer (SNS/SQS, Step Functions, Lambda) into a Bedrock-based RAG pipeline, with full output traceability, PII masking, and a 45-day retention window to satisfy SEC and insurance requirements.
  • Built model-level observability (Dynatrace on Bedrock invocations, Databricks for token usage) into a weekly C-suite performance review, with confidence-score drops routing back to Data Science and Engineering for retuning.

The results

  • Resolved a multi-month requirements deadlock in 3 weeks, restoring stakeholder trust and unblocking architecture work
  • Delivered $1.2M in annual savings through tiered model routing with no impact to user experience
  • Informed a $5M short-term investment in vendor migration, projected to yield ongoing annual savings

Full case study

The challenge

Haven Technologies had no engineering program management function: no governance, no delivery process, no cost structure. Engineering and product teams worked in silos, delivery capacity was in conflict, on-time delivery sat at 65%, and data infrastructure and cloud spend ran without a cost optimization owner.

The mission

Build the engineering operating system from zero (governance, delivery process, and cost optimization structure) that could take the company from startup to enterprise-grade, on-time delivery.

The approach

  • Founded and scaled the engineering program management function from the ground up, building an SDLC framework across 12 Product and Engineering teams.
  • Built a company-wide roadmap after siloed capacity conflicts caused missed deadlines, reprioritizing the backlog by risk and revenue impact and assigning ownership across every dependent system.
  • Introduced weekly tech syncs and a published Architecture Decision Record log capturing tradeoffs and constraints for every major call.
  • Owned end-to-end cost optimization across data infrastructure and cloud spend, including a CTO-directed Talend → Ab Initio ETL migration and the sunsetting of obsolete AWS services.

The results

  • On-time delivery improved from 65% to 96% over 2 years
  • $25M in annual licensing savings
  • $8M in AWS spend reduction, without sacrificing team velocity
  • 25% reduction in rework and late scope churn following ADR rollout
  • 96% of company-wide delivery goals hit in 2023

The challenge

Capital One faced an active AWS breach requiring immediate cybersecurity remediation under strict SEC compliance standards, at a time when no bank or financial services firm was fully on AWS. The response demanded coordination across Legal/Compliance, Platform and SRE Engineering spanning 48 concurrent initiatives with no real-time visibility into progress, risk or dependencies, at a moment when both accuracy and speed carried regulatory consequences.

The mission

Establish the governance, audit and accountability infrastructure necessary to achieve full SEC compliance, while reducing remediation cost and delivering enterprise-wide transparency into risk and delivery status at every organizational level.

The approach

  • Owned cross-functional delivery for the remediation program, coordinating Legal/Compliance, Platform and SRE Engineering toward full SEC compliance.
  • Partnered with Legal/Compliance to embed negotiated audit logging, access control and accountability standards directly into cloud infrastructure through the Cloud Center of Excellence.
  • Built a real-time productivity tracker spanning all 48 initiatives using JQL and Confluence, then partnered with a data scientist to add drill-down visualization reviewed daily, from C-suite down to individual contributors.
  • Identified cross-team risk and dependencies, producing velocity and burndown metrics via Tableau, JIRA, Confluence and Snowflake to inform mitigation plans.

The results

  • Reduced post-breach remediation spend by $22M, while achieving 100% SEC compliance
  • Delivered enterprise-wide, real-time visibility across 48 initiatives, adopted in daily organizational stand-ups

Expanding from single-product ownership of a content management system into cross-company integration and emerging tech exploration. Immediate priorities: shipping a classical AI-enabled CMS across 40 global offices and 100k+ users, restructuring pricing and packaging without hurting engagement, integrating Bureau van Dijk into Moody’s BI and risk tools post-acquisition, and stabilizing legacy AWS infrastructure. Throughout, I ran the work end to end myself alongside Engineering: requirements, testing, training and A/B testing.

The mission

Scale from single-product ownership to enterprise-wide platform delivery: ship the CMS globally, prove out a new pricing model with data, integrate an acquired company’s systems cleanly, and modernize infrastructure, without disrupting existing customers or engineering capacity.

The approach

  • Shipped a single-source AI-enabled CMS across 40 global offices (US, EMEA, APAC) to 100k+ paying users, driving the prioritization analysis that cut the delivery timeline by 50%, and serving as sole point of contact for training, feedback and rollout communications.
  • Led a buy-vs-build analysis recommending vendor acquisition over internal build, protecting engineering capacity and reducing capex exposure.
